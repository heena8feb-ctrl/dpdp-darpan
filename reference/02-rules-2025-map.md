## Rule 3 — Notice
The notice must:
- be **presented and understandable independently** of any other information the Data Fiduciary
  has made or may make available (i.e. not buried inside terms of service or a long privacy
  policy);
- give, in **clear and plain language**, a fair account of the details needed for **specific
  and informed consent**, including at minimum:
  - an **itemised description of the personal data**;
  - the **specified purpose**, with an **itemised description of the goods, services or uses**
    the processing enables;
- give the **communication link** to the website or app, and a description of other means, by
  which the Data Principal can:
  - **withdraw consent** with ease comparable to giving it;
  - **exercise rights** under the Act;
  - **complain to the Board**.
Language choice comes from s.5(3)/s.6(3): English or any Eighth Schedule language.

## Rule 4 + First Schedule — Consent Managers (from 13 Nov 2026)
- Must be a **company incorporated in India**, sound financial and operational capacity, **net
  worth of at least ₹2 crore**, reputation and fairness of management, platform independently
  certified against Board-published standards.
- Obligations include: interoperable platform; acting in a fiduciary capacity; **must not be
  able to read** the personal data it routes; maintain records of consents given, denied and
  withdrawn, notices and sharing, for **at least seven years** (or longer if agreed/required);
  give the Data Principal access to those records; no sub-contracting of obligations; avoid
  conflicts of interest with Data Fiduciaries; Board approval for transfer of control.
- Relevance to reviews: if a product plans to use a consent manager, check the manager is (or
  will be) registered; a Data Fiduciary must still honour consents and withdrawals routed through it.

## Rule 5 — State processing (subsidies, benefits, services)
Government and its instrumentalities processing under s.7(b) must follow Second Schedule
standards (lawful, purpose-limited, minimised, accurate, retention-limited, secure, with notice
and contact details). Relevant only for government/PSU flows.

## Rule 6 — Reasonable security safeguards
At minimum, appropriate technical and organisational measures including:
- **encryption, obfuscation, masking or virtual tokens** mapped to the personal data;
- **access control** to computer resources;
- **visibility on access** — logs, monitoring and review to detect unauthorised access, and to
  investigate, remediate and prevent recurrence;
- **continued processing** if confidentiality, integrity or availability is compromised — e.g.
  data backups;
- retaining such **logs and personal data for one year** for detection, investigation and
  remediation, unless another law requires otherwise;
- **contract provisions with processors** requiring them to take reasonable security safeguards;
- appropriate **technical and organisational measures** to ensure these are observed.
No specific standard (e.g. ISO 27001) or algorithm is mandated.

## Rule 7 — Breach intimation
- To **each affected Data Principal**, **without delay**, concisely, clearly and plainly,
  through their user account or registered mode of communication: description of the breach
  (nature, extent, timing, location); likely consequences relevant to them; mitigation measures
  taken or being taken; safety measures they can take; business contact of a person who can
  respond.
- To the **Board**: **without delay**, a description of the breach (nature, extent, timing,
  location, likely impact); and **within 72 hours** of becoming aware (or longer if the Board
  allows on written request): updated and detailed information — facts, circumstances,
  reasons, mitigation, findings about the person who caused it (if any), remedial measures to
  prevent recurrence, and a report on intimations given to Data Principals.
- No materiality threshold: every personal data breach is reportable.
- Runs alongside CERT-In's 6-hour incident reporting (see sector overlays).

## Rule 8 + Third Schedule — Erasure periods and minimum retention
- **Third Schedule classes** and erasure period (purpose deemed no longer served after the
  Data Principal has neither approached the Data Fiduciary for the purpose nor exercised rights
  for this period, counted from the latest of: last approach, last exercise of rights, or the
  Rules' commencement):
  - **E-commerce entity** with **≥ 2 crore** registered users in India — **3 years**.
  - **Online gaming intermediary** with **≥ 50 lakh** registered users in India — **3 years**.
  - **Social media intermediary** with **≥ 2 crore** registered users in India — **3 years**.
  - Exceptions apply (e.g. to let a user access their account or virtual tokens for money/credits
    held — verify the Schedule's exact carve-outs).
- **48-hour warning:** at least 48 hours before the period ends, inform the Data Principal that
  the data will be erased unless they log in, contact the Data Fiduciary or exercise rights.
- **Minimum retention (Rule 8(3)):** every Data Fiduciary must retain personal data, associated
  traffic data and other logs of processing for **at least one year** from the date of such
  processing, for the purposes specified in the Rules' schedule (e.g. security, investigation,
  compliance with Government information requests), after which they're erased unless another
  law requires retention. (The Rules include illustrations; verify exact scope.)
- Practical tension: s.8(7) says erase when purpose is served; Rule 8(3)/Rule 6 say keep
  processing logs/data for at least one year for specified purposes. Reconcile by **restricting**
  retained data to those purposes (segregated, access-controlled, not reused).

## Rule 9 — Contact information
Prominently publish on the website/app, and mention in every response to a communication for
exercise of rights, the business contact information of the **DPO** (if applicable) or a
person able to answer questions about processing.

## Rule 10 — Verifiable consent for a child
- Adopt **appropriate technical and organisational measures** to ensure verifiable consent of
  the parent is obtained, and **due diligence** that the person identifying as parent is an
  **identifiable adult**.
- Verify by reference to:
  - **reliable details of identity and age already available** with the Data Fiduciary; or
  - details **voluntarily provided** by the individual, or a **virtual token** mapped to such
    details, issued by an entity entrusted by law or the Government (e.g. a **Digital Locker**
    service provider).
- The Rules include illustrations covering: parent already a registered user; parent not a
  registered user; child declaring themselves an adult (verify details).

## Rule 11 — Verifiable consent for a person with disability
Due diligence that the guardian is a lawful guardian — appointed by a **court**, a **designated
authority** under the Rights of Persons with Disabilities Act, 2016, or a **local level
committee** under the National Trust Act, 1999.

## Rule 12 + Fourth Schedule — Children's data exemptions
Part A: classes of Data Fiduciaries (e.g. clinical establishments, mental health establishments,
healthcare professionals; allied healthcare professionals; educational institutions; individuals
in charge of crèches/child care; transport providers for children of educational institutions/
crèches) — exempt from s.9(1)/(3) **only to the extent necessary** for their listed purposes
(health services, educational activities, safety/tracking during transport, etc.).
Part B: purposes (e.g. exercising powers under law; State subsidies/benefits; creating a user
account limited to email communication; ensuring information likely to be detrimental isn't
accessible to the child; confirming the Data Principal isn't a child; real-time location for
safety) — exempt to the extent necessary.
Exact list and limits: verify against the Gazette text.

## Rule 13 — SDFs
- **DPIA and audit once every 12 months**, by a person carrying them out; report significant
  observations to the Board.
- **Due diligence** that algorithmic software used for processing (hosting, display, upload,
  modification, publishing, transmission, storage, update, sharing) isn't likely to pose a risk
  to Data Principals' rights.
- Ensure personal data **specified by the Government** (on a committee's recommendation) is
  processed subject to the restriction that it, and related traffic data, isn't transferred
  outside India.

## Rule 14 — Rights and grievances
- Publish prominently on website/app: the **means** by which a Data Principal can make a
  request (access, correction, erasure, nomination, grievance) and the **particulars** needed
  to identify them (e.g. username, customer ID).
- Publish the **grievance response period**, which must be **≤ 90 days**, and implement
  technical and organisational measures to meet it.
- Allow **nomination** of one or more individuals.

## Rule 15 — Transfer outside India
Personal data may be transferred abroad subject to requirements the Government specifies by
general or special order about making it available to a foreign State or entities under its
control.

## Rule 16 — Research, archiving, statistics
Processing for these purposes is exempt where carried out per Second Schedule standards
(lawful, purpose-limited, minimised, accurate, secure, no decisions specific to the individual).

## Rules 17–21 — Board
Selection committees, salaries, meetings, Board as a digital office, inquiries to be completed
within six months (extendable in three-month steps), staff terms.

## Rule 22 — Appeals
Appeals to the Appellate Tribunal (TDSAT) filed digitally.

## Rule 23 — Government calling for information
The Government may require a Data Fiduciary or intermediary to furnish information for
specified purposes (Seventh Schedule), and may direct non-disclosure where disclosure could
prejudice sovereignty, integrity or security of India.
